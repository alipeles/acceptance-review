## The design decision — settled, do not reopen

**Implementation coverage stops carrying and is re-derived for every requirement.**
Human's call at Gate 1, 2026-08-20.

The mandate requires the "was a cited file edited?" rule to be **removed** and the
two staleness axes decided **separately**, while a scope exclusion forbids
**narrowing** any stage other than test-evidence judgement. Deleting
`stale_obligation_ids` leaves coverage with no rule; the natural content-level
replacement is *narrower*, so it is excluded. Re-deriving everything widens rather
than narrows, and costs almost nothing because `classify_coverage` is a single
batched call (`coverage/classify.py:142`) — trimming its input shortens one prompt
and never removes a call.

The human noted that a content-level rule for coverage is the better answer and is
wanted **as a defence against instability, not as a cost saving** — deferred as a
future optimization, drafted in `docs/DEFERRED.md` as a sub-issue of #185.

## What is already built, which this extends

- **`src/acceptance/carry.py`** (#291, merged `5d1be23`) — the stage-agnostic
  reuse rule. `decide(...)` applies four checks and returns a `Decision` carrying
  a `Refusal` reason; `carry_key(...)` hashes the determinism controls plus the
  unit's own `inputs`. **#293 is its second consumer** — build the staleness
  predicate on this, not beside it. `carry_key` **spreads** `inputs` into the
  payload and must never nest them.
- **`src/acceptance/evidence/anchoring.py`** (#292) — `build_anchors` names a
  criterion's dependency changes at **file** granularity:

  **Sharpening this to content level is the heart of #293.** Read
  `docs/DR-292-anchored-rejudgement.md` first.
- **`judge_discrimination(..., anchors=...)`** rejects a rating move resting on
  no supplied change; `strength.py::hold_rejected_ratings` writes the held rating
  back.

**One rule from #292 to preserve:** a criterion with **no nameable change is not
anchored at all**. Anchoring it would freeze its rating forever.
