## Decisions the human made, 2026-09-22

- **Edits are built by `openai/gpt-5.4-mini`, not `openai/gpt-5.4`.** The
  recorded case against the smaller model is audit v2 against v5
  (`injection-audit-v5-judgement.md`): it returned 20+ unchanged edits, and
  `DR-171` added a check because 21 of 71 of its edits repeated their input
  verbatim. Both of those are now caught mechanically — #361's always-on refusal
  of comment- and whitespace-only edits — and retried, by #334's candidate loop.
  So the evidence that ruled the smaller model out predates the two mechanisms
  that answer it, and the cheap arm is worth measuring first.
- **Verification's two questions stay on `openai/gpt-5.4`**, through a per-stage
  override, because #335's bar was measured on that model
  (`verifier-measurement-gpt-5.4.md`). Moving both models at once would make the
  bar unanswerable. My decision, stated at the gate, not the human's.
- **One audit serves both issues.** Verification runs after the candidate loop,
  not inside it, so switching it on does not change which candidate is used.
- **Spend is approved stepwise.** A two-defect live smoke run first, to measure
  the cost per call, then the full run against a measured estimate rather than
  a guess.

## Do not mistake the ledger's derivation count for the obligation set

The ledger's `derivations[].obligations` records what each requirement derived,
before linking; the printed breakdown shows what survived merging. I once read
that gap as a hidden obligation and filed it as a blocker, which cost a withdrawn
Gate 1 confirmation. Check that every derived obligation id is either printed or
was merged away — not by subtracting merge decisions, which are recorded per pair.
