## The design question, answered

#340's Deliverable told `defects/support.py` to treat the new unjudged cause "the
way it treats a pair that was never seen, not the way it treats
`UnjudgedCause.PREFILTERED`". I verified those are the same arithmetic — in
`support.py:213-221` both add nothing to the `unknown` tally — so the instruction
could not be followed as written.

**The human's ruling, 2026-09-18:** a routed-out pair **raises `unknown`**, the
way `UNANSWERED` does. Plus a new rule: **when the judged pairs for a defect
yield no kill, that defect's skipped pairs go to the judge after all, before its
rating is derived.** Rule 3's reasoning arriving one step later — execution gave
nothing usable about that defect, so pay for the full judgement on it.

Both are now written into #340's Deliverable as amended rule 6 and a replaced
closing paragraph, with a matching Acceptance line.

  beside the existing `prefilter` call at `:556`.
- `defects/support.py:213` — the new cause joins `UNANSWERED` in raising
  `unknown`. Then rule 6: a defect whose judged pairs yield no kill has its
  skipped pairs judged **before** `derive_support` runs, which means the
  second judgement round sits in the pipeline, not inside `support.py`.
- `mutation/settings.py::decide_execution` — must stop returning `run=False` when
  `verify_edits` is off, since dropping pairs is now a second consumer of the run.
  Without this the tier never runs and the change does nothing.
- `report.py::_mutation_block` (`:293`) — per-defect settled / asked / dropped counts.
- `ExecutionSettings` — the on-by-default switch.

Rule 6 is the part the plan did not previously cover: it needs a second pass over
`judge_pairs` for the defects whose first pass came back with no kill, so the
pipeline's ordering around `judge_pairs` changes rather than just its input.
